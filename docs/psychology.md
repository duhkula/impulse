# Psychology and evidence-informed design

IMPULSE is an evidence-informed behavioural self-management tool. It is not intended to diagnose or treat ADHD or any mental-health condition.

## Rationale

The product is built around a practical behavioural hypothesis: when emotional activation increases the urge to act, introducing time, friction, private expression and alternative behaviours may help a person make a later choice that better reflects their longer-term intentions.

### Emotional dysregulation and ADHD

Research has described emotional dysregulation as a common and clinically relevant dimension associated with adult ADHD. IMPULSE therefore aims to minimise cognitive load during periods of high activation rather than relying on complex reflection or willpower.

### Impulsivity and immediate reward

Research on ADHD has found increased delay discounting on average: immediate outcomes may be weighted more heavily than delayed outcomes. This supports designing an intervention that makes the immediate action slightly harder and gives the user an immediate alternative outlet.

This must not be simplified into the claim that ADHD behaviour is simply caused by a “dopamine deficiency”. The app should avoid popular-neuroscience explanations that overstate the evidence.

### Thought suppression

Research on thought suppression has found evidence of rebound effects. IMPULSE therefore should not tell users to stop thinking about a person, subject or feeling. The intended approach is to acknowledge the thought and interrupt the behavioural chain that follows it.

## Product hypothesis

```text
Trigger
  ↓
Activation
  ↓
Fixation / rumination
  ↓
Urge
  ↓
Immediate action
  ↓
Short-term relief / stimulation
  ↓
Potential later consequences
```

IMPULSE attempts to insert:

```text
Urge
  ↓
PAUSE
  ↓
Private expression / alternative activity
  ↓
Reduced time pressure
  ↓
Choice
```

This is a product hypothesis, not a validated clinical model.

## Important limitations

- Not everyone with ADHD experiences emotional dysregulation.
- Emotional dysregulation and impulsivity are not unique to ADHD.
- A user's specific behaviour cannot be attributed to a single neurotransmitter or psychological mechanism without appropriate evidence.
- App blocking itself is not assumed to be clinically effective; it is a practical friction mechanism that should be evaluated with user testing.
- IMPULSE should measure whether users report that pauses help them, rather than making unsupported therapeutic claims.

## Design conclusion

The evidence does not justify claiming that IMPULSE is an ADHD treatment. It does support investigating a low-friction intervention that combines behavioural delay, private emotional expression and alternative actions.

The product should therefore be tested empirically and its claims kept proportional to the evidence.
